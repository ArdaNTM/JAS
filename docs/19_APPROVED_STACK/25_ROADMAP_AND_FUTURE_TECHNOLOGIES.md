# 1. PURPOSE

This document defines how JARVIS identifies, evaluates, experiments with, adopts, replaces, or rejects future technologies.

The purpose is not to predict the future perfectly.

The purpose is to ensure that:

> **When the future changes, JARVIS can evolve without losing architectural control.**

---

# 2. CORE PRINCIPLE

JARVIS must be designed for:

```text
Technological Change
```

without being designed around:

```text
Technological Instability
```

Therefore:

```text
Stable Core
+
Replaceable Components
+
Controlled Experimentation
=
Long-Term Evolution
```

---

# 3. ROADMAP IS NOT A WISHLIST

This document must not become a list of:

```text
Cool AI Technologies
Interesting GitHub Projects
Trending Frameworks
Random Research Papers
```

Instead, every future technology must have a relationship to:

```text
JAS
Current Stack
Known Limitation
Future Capability
Migration Cost
Risk
```

---

# 4. FUTURE TECHNOLOGY DEFINITION

A future technology is any technology that:

```text
Is not currently part of the production Approved Stack
```

---

# 20. WATCH

The technology is monitored but no active implementation is planned.

---

# 21. INVESTIGATE

The technology has enough potential to justify deeper research.

---

# 22. CANDIDATE

The technology has passed initial research and should be benchmarked.

---

# 23. EXPERIMENTAL

A controlled prototype is being developed.

---

# 24. PILOT

The technology is being tested in a realistic JARVIS environment.

---

# 25. ADOPT

The technology has been approved for inclusion in the production Approved Stack.

---

# 26. DEFER

The technology is valuable but adoption is currently premature.

---

# 27. REJECT

The technology was evaluated and should not be adopted under current conditions.

---

# 28. ARCHIVED

The technology is no longer considered strategically relevant.

---

# 564. TECHNOLOGY RADAR

The roadmap uses a separate Technology Radar to distinguish:

```text
ADOPT
TRIAL
ASSESS
HOLD
```

The radar is a decision-state layer. It does not replace the formal:

```text
Research
↓
Evaluation
↓
Experiment
↓
Pilot
↓
Approval
↓
Version Lock
↓
Production
```

A radar state must never be interpreted as production authorization by itself.

---

# 565. RADAR — ADOPT

```text
ADOPT
```

means the technology has demonstrated sufficient value and compatibility to enter the Approved Stack change process.

ADOPT requires, at minimum:

```text
Measured Benefit
+
Supported Lifecycle
+
Security Review
+
License Review
+
Integration Validation
+
Operational Verification
```

The exact production version remains controlled by Version Lock.

---

# 566. RADAR — TRIAL

```text
TRIAL
```

means the technology is sufficiently promising for a controlled JARVIS pilot.

TRIAL technologies must remain isolated from critical production paths until the pilot passes its predefined acceptance criteria.

---

# 567. RADAR — ASSESS

```text
ASSESS
```

means the technology has enough strategic relevance to justify structured investigation, benchmarking, or prototype work.

ASSESS does not imply implementation commitment.

---

# 568. RADAR — HOLD

```text
HOLD
```

means the technology is known and potentially relevant, but there is currently insufficient evidence, maturity, strategic need, or migration justification to invest further.

HOLD is not the same as REJECT.

---

# 569. RADAR DECISION RULE

The Technology Radar should answer:

```text
Where should JARVIS spend engineering attention?
```

It must not answer:

```text
What is already approved for production?
```

Production authorization remains governed by the Approved Stack, Version Support Policy, Version Lock, Manifest, Bootstrap, and Verification chain.

---

# 570. RADAR TRANSITION RULES

Radar movement should normally follow evidence:

```text
HOLD
  ↓
ASSESS
  ↓
TRIAL
  ↓
ADOPT
```

A candidate may also move directly to:

```text
HOLD
  ↓
REJECT
```

or:

```text
ASSESS / TRIAL
  ↓
HOLD
```

when evidence no longer justifies continued investment.

No technology may move to ADOPT solely because of popularity, benchmark hype, funding, or ecosystem size.
